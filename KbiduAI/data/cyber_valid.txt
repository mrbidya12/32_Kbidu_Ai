The OWASP Top 10 includes Injection, Broken Authentication, Sensitive Data Exposure, and other critical security risks.

Defense in depth is a security strategy that layers multiple defensive mechanisms to protect systems and data, so if one layer fails, others still provide protection.

Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) is a system that creates, manages, distributes, and revokes digital certificates.

A certificate authority (CA) is a trusted entity that issues digital certificates used to verify the identity of individuals or organizations.

Digital signatures provide authentication, integrity, and non-repudiation for digital documents and transactions.

Hashing algorithms like SHA-256 are used to verify data integrity by producing a unique fixed-size hash for any given input.

Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) and Transport Layer Security (TLS) are cryptographic protocols that provide secure communication over a computer network.

The principle of least privilege ensures that users only have the access rights necessary to perform their job functions.

Identity and Access Management (IAM) is the framework of policies and technologies that ensure the right individuals access the right resources at the right times for the right reasons.

Privileged Access Management (PAM) focuses on securing, controlling, and monitoring access to privileged accounts.
